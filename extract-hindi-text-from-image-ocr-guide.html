What is OCR?

OCR software analyses the shapes in an image and attempts to recognise letters, numbers and words. It is useful for printed notices, forms, book pages and scanned documents. OCR is not a perfect copy process: low resolution, unusual fonts, shadows, handwriting and mixed scripts can cause recognition errors.

Prepare an image for better recognition

  • Use a sharp image with readable text and enough light.
  • Keep the page straight and avoid perspective distortion.
  • Crop out unrelated background while keeping punctuation and line endings.
  • Choose an image where text contrasts clearly with the background.
  • For Hindi and English on the same page, select a combined language option when available.

Accuracy tip: A clear, high-resolution printed page is usually easier to recognise than a blurred photo or handwriting. Always proofread names, dates, amounts and official identifiers.

What OCR may get wrong

Similar characters

Some letters and digits look alike, especially in small or low-quality images.

Matras and punctuation

Hindi vowel marks, conjuncts and punctuation may need manual correction.

Columns and tables

Reading order can differ from the original page layout.

Handwriting

Handwritten text may be recognised less reliably than clear printed text.

Privacy and responsible use

Frequently asked questions

Can KAGZI read Hindi text from an image?

KAGZI Image Text Editor offers Hindi OCR and a Hindi + English language option. Recognition quality depends on image clarity and the printed text.

Can OCR read handwriting accurately?

Handwriting recognition can be unreliable. The tool is best treated as an aid for clear printed text, and all results should be proofread.

Can I edit the recognised text?

The editor displays recognised text items and provides controls to inspect and edit text in the image workflow. Review the exported image carefully.

Does OCR guarantee a perfect transcription?

No. OCR can confuse similar characters, punctuation, matras, digits and reading order. Verify important content against the source image.
